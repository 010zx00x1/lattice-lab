# Features and technical limits

This document describes the implemented behavior and its practical limits. Lattice Lab is an exploratory prototype. Its geometry checks, material data, and simulation results are inputs to review, not proof that a part is safe or manufacturable.

## Import and preparation

- Import STL, STEP, and 3MF, or start from six built-in examples: a hinge bracket, perforated angle bracket, connecting rod, rocker, cube, and sphere. The four mechanical examples are original browser-generated meshes, not STEP files or copies of IMPLICIT samples.
- Inspect triangle orientation, degenerate faces, open edges, non-manifold edges, and volume. Small flat convex holes can be repaired. Generation is blocked for an open, non-manifold, or zero-volume source.
- Manage multiple bodies from STEP or 3MF by hiding, restoring, or removing them. Only visible bodies participate in analysis and lattice generation. If repair changes triangle counts so body ranges cannot be preserved safely, the editor presents one repaired mesh.
- STEP conversion can preserve B-rep face triangle ranges supplied by `occt-import-js` when repair does not change the triangle count. For STL/3MF, or after topology-changing repair, neighboring triangles are grouped by normal angle; this geometric grouping does not recover original CAD faces.
- Create, rename, edit, and remove groups of up to 100 detected faces. Groups can be assigned to supports, loads, solid reinforcement, and openings in the outer skin. Changing the face-detection angle or body topology clears groups because stored face indices are no longer reliable.
- Scale the loaded mesh uniformly and recompute dimensions, volume, and faces. For example, a mesh modeled in inches can be multiplied by 25.4. The saved project stores the scaled geometry and the scale factor.
- Current input limit: 25 MB and 80,000 triangles. Cell size is adjusted to the scale of the part.

## Lattice geometry

- TPMS families: Gyroid, Schwarz P, Diamond, Schoen I-WP, Neovius, Fischer–Koch S, Schoen F-RD, and Fischer–Koch C(Y). Periodic beam families: Cubic, BCC, FCC, Octahedron, and Octet. They are sampled as fields and clipped to the part. Five newer TPMS fields use nodal approximations from [this research](https://iris.unige.it/retrieve/a7f520d5-dfe4-435a-a941-7c9feca324a1/phdunige_4963071.pdf); they are not exact minimal surfaces or mechanically equivalent to the reference product.
- Each TPMS can be generated as a sheet around its nodal surface or as a solid network filling one labyrinth. A network does not have uniform wall thickness: the wall control moves the field threshold and changes material fraction. Suggested connected settings for I-WP, Neovius, and F-RD were checked on a test cube; a new part still needs connectivity review.
- Stochastic options include closed-cell Voronoi foam, bars around Voronoi edges, bars between neighboring sites as a Delaunay approximation, and a Spinodal-like field built from seeded oblique waves. The seed makes results reproducible. The Delaunay bars are not exact tetrahedralization, and the Spinodal field does not simulate phase separation. An outer skin can connect bars that are separated after clipping. Thin features require enough sampling resolution and inspection of the exported mesh.
- An optional outer skin preserves a band near the source surface using an approximate grid distance. It does not guarantee a local minimum thickness. Selected face openings suppress the solid band near those regions so the lattice can reach the boundary. The transition depends on sampling resolution.
- Skin can cover the whole exterior except selected openings, or only selected faces. An optional cylindrical rim follows selected face boundaries. The final mesh is validated for faulty joins. Fine skins on large parts can create hundreds of thousands of triangles and take tens of seconds.
- Up to eight prioritized spherical or box-shaped local zones change relative wall thickness. Each has a center, size, target thickness, and transition width; zones are saved in the project.
- Optional solid zones preserve material near selected support and load faces. Their depth is sampled from the distance to selected triangles and is resolution dependent. Skin and solid zones can be combined, but the parametric search requires both to be off.
- Equidistant layers add perforated bands at cell-size intervals from the outside. Conformal cells shift phase by depth from the first surface hit along the print axis. Each mode is validated after generation; their combination is disabled because it produced non-manifold edges in a tested case. Conformal mode is disabled for the sphere, whose silhouette degenerates that mapping. CAD walls nearly parallel to the axis can distort it.
- Beam and Voronoi modes adapt sampling step to nominal feature thickness to reduce missing thin elements. Check actual connectivity and thickness in the export.
- The generated mesh is cleaned and validated before export. The report distinguishes closed mesh surfaces from connected material regions on the voxel grid: a hollow skin can have inner and outer surfaces yet form one material region. Grid connectivity is approximate. Significant separated regions are reported, but export remains available for review.

## Material and manufacturing checks

- Estimated mass combines source volume, sampled material reduction, and the selected density. [Prusament PLA](https://prusament.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/PLA_Prusament_TDS_2021_10_EN.pdf) and [Formlabs Nylon 12](https://formlabs.com/eu/materials/tds/nylon-12-powder/) profiles show manufacturer-published density, modulus, and strength. The cited Nylon 12 value is ultimate strength, not yield strength. Initial thermal conductivity is illustrative. Other PLA, PETG, ABS, aluminum, and steel profiles are explicitly generic.
- [Original Prusa MK4S](https://www.prusa3d.com/product/original-prusa-mk4s-3d-printer-kit/) and [Formlabs Fuse 1+ 30W](https://formlabs.com/3d-printers/fuse-1/tech-specs/) profiles test whether the part's bounding box fits the nominal build volume in the selected print direction, allowing a 90° in-plane rotation. A part outside that volume is blocked with the selected machine. Real clearances, shrinkage compensation, and corner access must be checked in machine software.
- Beam, Voronoi foam, and stochastic-bar nominal diameters are checked against the user-selected minimum; generation is blocked if they fall below it. After generation, up to 640 inward rays are cast from area-distributed surface triangles using [three-mesh-bvh](https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-mesh-bvh/blob/master/API.md). The editor reports coverage, observed minimum, and fifth percentile (P5) of directional thickness. Export is blocked if P5 is below the requested minimum or fewer than 85% of rays find an opposing wall. A passing sample does not establish the global minimum; inspect STL/3MF in manufacturing software.
- The saved print direction and maximum overhang angle feed a worker that measures downward-facing triangle area beyond the angle, excluding build-plate contact facets. The indicator updates when orientation changes, without regenerating the lattice. It does not calculate support structures, access, bridging, bed adhesion, or print success.
- Material reduction is a sampled volume estimate. The generation estimate reports grid nodes and minimum field memory, and the editor reports actual calculation time. Triangulation and validation require additional memory. Generation, search, and simulation can be cancelled.
- WebGL results above 250,000 triangles default to an 80,000-point preview with a control to show the complete mesh. The Canvas 2D fallback also samples dense meshes and lowers detail during interaction. Analysis, simulation, and export use the full result.

## Local projects and editing

- **Save project** downloads JSON with the source mesh and material, lattice, solid-zone, and simulation settings. Multi-body projects preserve each mesh and its visibility. Older single-body projects still open. **Open project** restores data and revalidates the active source mesh; regenerate the lattice afterward.
- Parts are not uploaded or automatically saved. Project JSON may be large and contains the original part geometry.
- Undo/redo buttons and Ctrl+Z/Ctrl+Y outside text fields cover up to 50 changes to lattice parameters, zones, and skin-face selections. History resets when a part or project is loaded. It does not include geometry, body, material, or simulation-result changes. A four-step Guide covers the main workflow.

## Structural analysis

- The lattice is sampled on a grid of at most 20 voxels along its longest axis, with eight samples per voxel and a majority threshold. Only the largest face-connected solid component is kept.
- Each solid voxel becomes an eight-node trilinear hexahedral element integrated at eight Gauss points. The model is linear, isotropic elasticity with Poisson ratio 0.30. A preconditioned conjugate-gradient solver computes nodal displacement.
- In automatic mode, all three displacements are fixed on the minimum face of the selected axis and force is distributed to maximum-face nodes. For two selected geometric regions, their triangles are projected onto grid nodes; support nodes are fixed and load acts along the centroid-to-centroid direction in tension or compression. Missing or overlapping regions stop the calculation. A central per-element von Mises stress is projected to XY; a P95 indicator limits the influence of a single voxel.
- Loads can be total force, approximate uniform pressure, or a moment about the load direction. Pressure is multiplied by selected-face area, or by bounding-box face area in automatic mode. A moment is distributed as a nodal couple with zero net force. Up to 16 cases with independent objective, faces, load mode, and parameters are saved in a project.
- This is an exploratory estimate. Coarse voxelization changes thickness, connections, and selected-face shape. Do not use it to size or certify a part. Check mesh convergence and real material properties in engineering software.

## Thermal and channel analysis

- Thermal analysis reuses the voxel grid and largest face-connected component. It solves steady conduction with cell-centered finite volumes, converting W/(m·K) to W/(mm·K), with link conductance `kA/L`. The cold face is fixed at a chosen temperature; power is distributed at the opposite face. Other faces are insulated. There is no convection, radiation, internal heat source, or temperature-dependent material behavior. A solid reference bar reproduces the one-dimensional Fourier solution; the voxel model remains too coarse for thermal certification.
- Channel analysis voxelizes the source and lattice together. Channel cells are inside the source and outside the lattice. It finds channel components touching both chosen ends, solves a normalized pressure network with geometric conductance `face area / center distance`, and compares against the unfilled source. It reports connected-channel share, relative conductance, and pressure from 1 to 0. This is **not CFD**: it does not calculate flow rate, velocity, viscosity, turbulence, or wall losses. Thin channels may disappear at the chosen grid resolution.

## Search, library, and grading

- Parametric search evaluates up to 15 cell-size and TPMS wall settings. It tests low-material candidates first, rejects strongly fragmented fields, and runs the exploratory elastic solver until a candidate meets a displacement target. Applying a suggestion generates and validates the actual mesh, then reruns the structural estimate. Search displacement is a grid estimate, not a guarantee for the final mesh. Search does not move material freely or perform general topology optimization.
- `scripts/calibrate-library.mjs` reproduces 57 periodic test cases on a 20 mm cube with 10 mm cells, an axial X load, a 1000 MPa isotropic material, and grids of 12 and 16 voxels per axis. Each voxel uses eight samples. Cases that fail to converge or lose over 2% of connected material are excluded. The editor searches this catalog by relative density or effective axial stiffness. Stiffness scales linearly with the chosen material modulus; stiffness suggestions must meet the target at the lower end of the two-resolution range. The range represents grid sensitivity, not statistical uncertainty or a certified property of a real part.
- **Analyze solid part** solves an exploratory elastic case on the source. Stress-guided wall grading interpolates its P95 field and varies wall thickness between chosen bounds; higher-stress regions receive more material. Stress-guided cell sizing reduces local cell size where stress rises for TPMS and beams. Local period changes approximate a conformal field and can distort phase or create small connections that require export review. Neither method optimizes topology or guarantees a stiffness target. Uniform parametric search is disabled while grading is active. The project saves both grading settings and recomputes the source stress field when reopened to avoid stale results.

Lattice Lab does not implement free-form topology optimization or full fluid simulation.
