# Functional parity and validation

The goal is an independent workflow comparable to the publicly usable features of [implicit.build](https://implicit.build/) observed on October 22, 2026. This project does not include its brand, sample files, proprietary code, or announced future Pro features. A task is marked complete only after a usable behavior and result were checked; a visible control alone does not count. Matching labels or appearance is not evidence of equivalent geometry or simulation.

## Checklist

- [x] **01. Inventory and criteria.** Record the public Prepare → optional Simulate → Lattice → Export flow, feature families, and differences. Evidence came from the public browser interface. REA recorded the public bundle, but static analysis could not finish because of memory limits.
- [x] **02. Sample parts.** Provide original mechanical parts with distinct shapes and holes, plus primitives. All six are closed meshes; the four mechanical parts generate valid lattices with their initial settings.
- [x] **03. CAD import and preparation.** Test real STEP, STL, and 3MF files; preserve bodies, units, and scale; allow hiding/removing bodies, changing scale, and mesh diagnosis/repair without misrepresenting CAD faces.
- [x] **04. Material and manufacturing.** Select technology, material, and machine; show density, modulus, published strength, and sources; apply indicative thickness, orientation, and build-volume limits. Final-mesh thickness is sampled by rays and does not certify a global minimum.
- [x] **05. Geometric regions.** Create and edit face groups; assign them to supports, loads, solid zones, and openings; preserve assignments across project save/load. Face indices become invalid and groups are cleared when detection angle or body topology changes.
- [x] **06. Before/after simulation.** Keep independent load cases and support/force/pressure/moment conditions. Provide verifiable structural, thermal, and channel analyses while stating model limits. These modes are exploratory approximations; no other public modes have been validated here.
- [x] **07. Cell library.** Generate Gyroid, Diamond, Schwarz P, Schoen I-WP, Neovius, Fischer–Koch S, Schoen F-RD, and Fischer–Koch C(Y) in available forms; five periodic beams; Voronoi foam and bars; approximate Delaunay bars and Spinodal surfaces. Each option generated at least one closed exportable solid. Delaunay and Spinodal approximations are labeled in the editor.
- [x] **08. Spatial lattice control.** Support whole or selected-face skins, openings and rims, up to eight local wall zones, stress-guided wall and cell sizing, equidistant layers, and conformal layouts where geometry permits. Joins are validated after generation. Combining layers with conformal layout and applying conformal layout to the sphere remain blocked after observed degeneracies.
- [x] **09. Objective library.** Compare 57 periodic configurations calibrated at two voxel resolutions; search by density or axial stiffness; show the resolution range and apply family, form, and wall control. Modulus scales to the selected isotropic material. The final part still requires generation and its own simulation.
- [x] **10. Performance and resolution.** Use point previews for dense results with and without WebGL, retain the complete export mesh, estimate grid memory and elapsed time, support cancellation, and enforce safe input limits. Measured in headless Firefox with a 1,017,420-triangle result; other devices may differ.
- [x] **11. Export and manufacturing.** Export STL and 3MF in millimeters; inspect closure, connectivity, sampled thickness, and overhangs on the final mesh; retain warnings and round-trip tests of exported bytes. These are exploratory checks, not manufacturing certification.
- [ ] **12. Project experience.** Save/open all required parameters and geometry; provide undo/redo, a guide, help, and languages visible in the reference. Keyboard behavior and accessibility still need review. The current interface is Spanish only.
- [ ] **13. Final comparison.** Run the same example and user-provided parts in both products; compare geometry, mass, time, and simulation against documented tolerances. Publish a parity claim only after the acceptance matrix passes.

## Verified observations

- **Import:** A binary STL cube (40 × 40 × 40 mm), an official `occt-import-js` STEP cube (300 × 300 × 300 mm), and an inch-unit 3MF cube (converted to 25.4 × 25.4 × 25.4 mm) loaded as closed meshes in the browser. Manual 2× scale updated dimensions and mass. The STEP retained six B-rep faces of 90,000 mm² for selection. A two-body 3MF allowed hide/restore/remove while retaining a valid mesh. The official STEP assembly `as1-oc-214.stp` imported 18 bodies and remained at 17 closed components after one body was hidden. Version 2 projects save multiple bodies; version 1 remains readable.
- **Regions:** A cube face group was created, edited, assigned as a fixed support for source analysis, and another group was used as a skin opening. Project round-tripping retained names, faces, and support, load, opening, and solid-zone assignments. Groups combine detected regions; freehand triangle painting is not implemented.
- **Manufacturing:** FDM/SLS technologies, sourced Prusament and Formlabs material profiles, two published machine build volumes, and generic profiles were added. A 300 mm STEP part was blocked by the Fuse 1+ nominal 165 × 165 × 300 mm volume. A beam below the selected nominal minimum diameter was blocked before generation. Build-volume checks use the bounding box, not PreForm or PrusaSlicer. Final geometry is measured with up to 640 inward rays accelerated by [three-mesh-bvh](https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-mesh-bvh/blob/master/API.md). A 40 mm Gyroid cube produced 640/640 ray hits, P5 thickness 1.39 mm, and observed minimum 0.26 mm; setting a 1.5 mm minimum disabled STL export. P5 is a sampled gate, not proof of a global minimum.
- **Simulation:** A solid cube was analyzed before lattice generation. Up to 16 independent cases are saved in a project. In the browser, 0.1 MPa pressure on a 40 mm cube became 160 N, and a 100 N·mm moment produced displacement and stress; switching back to an earlier case restored its load type and magnitude. A force-balance test checked that the nodal couple has zero net force and requested moment; elastic response scales linearly with moment. Pressure uses selected-face or bounding-box area, and the channel model uses geometric conductance rather than CFD.
- **Cells:** All eight TPMS families produced closed, connected sheet and network meshes with tuned cube settings. F-RD network produced 122,040 triangles, one connected material region, and an estimated 37% volume reduction. Five beam families, Voronoi foam and bars, approximate Delaunay bars, and approximate Spinodal generated closed connected material in a 24 mm cube with a 1.2 mm skin. Without skin, Delaunay bars may remain separated, which the editor warns about. Exact geometric equivalence with IMPLICIT is not claimed.
- **Export:** A generated Gyroid lattice was serialized to binary STL and 3MF, then read back with closure, triangle count, and millimeter dimensions preserved. A known 2 mm hollow wall returned P5 = 2 mm. Samples can miss localized defects, and the observed minimum may include noisy intersections.
- **Spatial controls:** Equidistant perforated layers and conformal cells separately generated closed connected meshes in a 24 mm cube; conformal mapping also passed with an imported sloped-cap part. The browser reported 151,318 triangles for layers and 167,898 for conformal cells. Their combination produced a non-manifold edge and is blocked. A sphere silhouette degenerates conformal mapping and is rejected. A local wall zone on a 40 mm cube produced a closed mesh of 62,736 triangles and one connected material region. Selected-face skin passed in the browser; a rim passed a closed-mesh test on a 24 mm cube. Selected-face skin on a 40 mm cube generated 949,760 triangles in 32 seconds, illustrating the performance cost.
- **Objective library:** `scripts/calibrate-library.mjs` tests 13 families on 20 mm cubes with relative wall control 0.20–0.44 and 12- and 16-voxel grids, excluding nonconverged or disconnected cases. The editor sorts 57 valid cases by density or conservative axial stiffness. Schwarz P network at wall control 0.20 showed 40.7–50.0% relative density and 364–591 MPa for Prusament PLA. This is grid sensitivity, not a statistical interval or certified property. The browser applied family, form, and thickness from a suggestion.
- **Project experience:** The editor keeps up to 50 states of lattice, zone, and skin-face parameters. In the browser, a wall-control change from 0.26 to 0.32 was undone and redone. The four-step Guide was completed. Geometry import and simulation results are outside undo history; localization and an accessibility audit remain open.
- **Performance:** Without WebGL, an automated drag of a 122,040-triangle F-RD result took 271 ms; generation took 3.6 seconds. With WebGL2 in headless Firefox, a 1,017,420-triangle F-RD took 35.6 seconds to generate. The full mesh produced only three draw frames during an approximately 599 ms automated drag, with a 359 ms maximum pause. An 80,000-point preview produced 31 frames over 608 ms, with an estimated mean 18.4 ms and maximum 36 ms interval, grouped from draw calls. The point view is an approximation and the complete export mesh is retained. These measurements do not guarantee performance on other hardware.
